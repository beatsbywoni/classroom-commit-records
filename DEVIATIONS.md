# Deviation and decision log

## Registered implementation decisions (D1–D10, recorded 2026-08-06 before outcomes were observed)

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Signals are computed on human-authored commits only (bot regex as registered) | The construct is student behavior; Classroom template commits would contaminate edit size and large-insert measures |
| D2 | Merge commits (two or more parents) are excluded from line-based signals, included in timing signals | `git log --numstat` records no diff for merges; including them would bias line-based signals toward zero |
| D3 | If an era × language cohort has fewer than 100 commits, its P95 threshold falls back to the corpus-wide P95; thresholds published | Unstable thresholds in small cohorts |
| D4 | Trailer lines (Co-authored-by, Signed-off-by, …) are stripped before message embedding; messages truncated to 256 characters | Trailer text is tool output, not student language; including it would mechanically correlate similarity with the AI label |
| D5 | Message informativeness is defined only for repositories with two or more messages | Pairwise cosine is undefined otherwise |
| D6 | Time dispersion is defined only for four or more commits with non-zero span | Quantiles require it |
| D7 | Single-repository students: organization × era cohort z; cohorts with fewer than 5 repositories fall back to era-level z (undefined below 30) | Unstable standardization in small cohorts |
| D8 | H3 (widening gap) uses organization × era cohort z rather than within-student z | Within-student standardization removes between-student baseline variance, making the moderation test undefined |
| D9 | The year variance component is excluded from era tests (H1, P1, boundary shifts) and kept for trailer tests | Era is nested within calendar year; a year component absorbs the era contrast |
| D10 | H3 falls back to OLS with organization-clustered SE if the student random intercept is singular | Most multi-repository students have exactly one later observation |

D1, D2, D4, D5 and D6 carry over to the corrected analysis unchanged. D3 and D7–D10 apply only to the registered analysis.

## Corrected-analysis decisions (E1–E6, recorded 2026-10-30 after the registered results had been observed and the standardization flaw diagnosed; see Appendix A of the paper)

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Signals are analyzed on their raw scales; no within-student or within-cohort standardization. Standardized effects are β / SD(y) with SD from the analysis sample | Within-cohort standardization set the era mean of every signal to zero in each organization × era cell for 71% of the registered sample, removing the era contrast by construction |
| E2 | Large-insert share uses absolute thresholds (≥ 200 added lines; ≥ 500 and log of largest single insertion as sensitivity) | Era-specific P95 thresholds classify 5% of commits as large in every era and cannot detect change; empirical P95 values (up to 209,561 lines for C/C++) reflect vendored files, not pastes |
| E3 | The AI-paste co-occurrence signal is removed | It is identically zero in repositories without AI trailers, making any trailer contrast on a composite containing it partly circular |
| E4 | Primary sample = repositories verified as GitHub Classroom-generated (description "created by GitHub Classroom" or `github-classroom` in the name), at least one human author and at least three human commits, excluding organizations whose names mark them as test/demo/sandbox/sample/playground/staging/production instances. Sensitivity: five-commit threshold; test organizations included; organization-owned likely-coursework repositories added; full frame | Only 18% of the registered sample was Classroom-generated; the rest were "classroom"-named projects, 72% owned by individuals, so the organization random intercept had no meaning. GitHub's own product-test organizations were in the sample |
| E5 | Signals are reported individually with Holm correction per family of seven; a pooled-z composite is shown once as an exhibit and not used for inference | The signals move in opposite directions; a composite reports their cancellation as stability. The registered convergent-validity test had already failed |
| E6 | Trailer contrast restricted to first commits from 2024-01-01 and run on the full frame (too few trailer repositories in the primary sample) and repeated on the primary sample; placebo = human Co-authored-by trailers; AI and human indicators enter one model and the AI − human difference is Wald-tested with Holm correction | AI trailers are a 2025–2026 phenomenon; Signed-off-by is too rare in that window (41 repositories) to serve as a placebo; a placebo's non-significance is not evidence of specificity, the difference is |
| E7 (2026-10-30, after the first corrected analysis had been run and found to depend on one organization) | Every contrast is reported under three estimators: raw, organization random intercepts, and organizations weighted equally (each organization capped at 50 randomly drawn repositories, 40 draws, Rubin-style variance combination); leave-one-organization-out over the ten largest organizations and with the five largest removed. Claims rest on agreement (robust / suggestive / not supported rule in Section 5.1 of the paper) | Removing the largest organization (763 repositories; 762 of the 2,046 spanning both eras; 545 of the 682 spanning the agentic boundary) reversed the sign of two of three random-intercept era effects and eliminated the agentic-era effects |
| E8 (2026-10-30) | Within-organization fixed-effects inference uses a wild cluster bootstrap (Rademacher, 1,999 replications, null imposed) instead of cluster-robust standard errors; organization-level paired differences with a sign test are reported alongside | 15 and 12 clusters, one holding 37% / 80% of observations; cluster-robust SEs were far too small (e.g., same-file reruns p = 1.6e-15 with d = −0.07) |
| E9 (2026-10-30, after an author work order questioned whether non-bot commits are student commits) | Signals are computed on student commits only: non-bot commits that are not *inherited* (a commit SHA appearing in two or more repositories of the same organization, i.e., template or fork history) and not by a *staff-like* author (an author with commits in five or more repositories of the organization who is the top committer in fewer than half of them). Inclusion requires three or more student commits. Same-file reruns are recomputed on student commits (`18_arcs_student.py`). Era is classified by the first student commit (changes era for 1 repository) | In verified Classroom repositories with three or more non-bot commits, 10% of pre-era non-bot commits (and 25% of pre-era repositories) carried inherited template/fork history, concentrated in 2017–2019 when Classroom created repositories by forking; post-era share 1%. Treating these as student commits inflates pre-era commit counts and edit sizes |
| E10 (2026-10-30) | Repositories with one or two student commits are analyzed separately on the signals computable from them (edit size, large-insert share, largest insertion, test-path touches); the distribution of student commits (0/1/2/3+) by era is reported so that the inclusion rule's selectivity is visible | A pasting change could concentrate in repositories with very few commits, which the three-commit rule excludes |
| E11 (2026-10-30) | Year effects are compared by direct contrasts (2023 vs 2022) using the coefficient covariance; effects are also reported in natural units (percentage points, lines); the AI/human trailer model includes year fixed effects and the primary-sample difference is tested with a wild cluster bootstrap; estimands are stated explicitly (average repository / average organization / within-organization change) and the capped estimator is presented as a weighting that limits large organizations, not as a cure for era–organization confounding | Author work order |
| E12 (2026-10-30, after an audit of the analysis by a separate model session; see AI_USE.md) | The capped estimator is also run with caps of 25 and 100 (20 draws each) and an OLS model with the covariates but no organization term is reported; the consistency criterion requires the same sign at all three caps; language family is added to every within-organization fixed-effects model; the capped estimator is repeated across samples (≥5 student commits, test organizations retained, tier A+B, full frame ≥3) and thresholds (`20_sample_sensitivity.py`); the share of verified repositories with no student commit is reported by creation year (`19_inclusion_all.py`). Correction: the within-organization trailer difference in `16_composition_v2.py` had been parameterized as y ~ (T−H) + T, whose first coefficient is −β_H rather than β_AI − β_H; it is now y ~ T + (T+H), whose coefficient on T is β_AI − β_H, and the individual AI and human p-values are written to the results file | The cap's value is a choice; reporting one cap would present a weighting as an answer. Under the degenerate fits later corrected by E13, the caps appeared to change the sign of the commit-size estimate (edit size +0.09 at 25, −0.21 at 50, −0.24 at 100); after E13 the capped commit-size estimates are near zero at every cap (+0.04, −0.02, −0.07) |
| E13 (2026-10-30, after a second audit by a separate model session) | Every mixed model is fitted by REML with the BFGS optimizer, with Powell and Nelder–Mead as fallbacks, and a fit is accepted only if it converged with a finite log-likelihood and finite standard errors (`mfit()` in `14_figures_v2.py`, `16_composition_v2.py`, `20_sample_sensitivity.py`; the count of fits by optimizer is written to each results file as `fit_log`) | statsmodels' L-BFGS optimizer, used until then, returned degenerate random-intercept fits on these data: infinite log-likelihood, a reported organization variance of zero, and fixed effects identical to the organization fixed-effects solution (era edit size: L-BFGS −0.451, equal to fixed effects; BFGS, Powell, Nelder–Mead all −0.280 with organization variance 1.35 and log-likelihood −10,971.7). Every random-intercept and capped estimate was recomputed. The registered analysis (`08_analysis.sh`) and the first corrected analysis (`13_analysis_v2.py`) also used L-BFGS; their results are retained as produced |
| E14 (2026-10-30, after the author's sixth work order asked whether the definitions and checks supported the conclusions) | (a) Activity rule v4 replaces the rule of E9: a non-bot commit is classified as student activity unless it was committed more than one day before the repository was created, or shares its SHA with another repository of the organization and was committed before creation (pre-existing history); or shares its SHA with five or more repositories of the organization and was committed after creation (push to many repositories); or its author meets the staff-like criterion (five or more repositories, top contributor in fewer than half) and does not match the repository name (`23_flags_v4.py`). The main results are also reported under the previous rule and six variants (no one-day margin; all shared SHAs excluded; staff-like at three and at ten repositories; no staff-like rule; owner-matched commits only). The rules were checked against commit timing and author identity (`22_activity_audit.py`) and against a stratified review of 144 commits (rule outcome × creation period × organization size; `29_activity_review_v4.py`); the review judgments were made first by the language model under a written protocol and then checked by the author, and are kept, with commit subjects, in an access-controlled file. Because the review uses the rule's own inputs plus the subject line, it is reported as a metadata-based check that finds clear errors and their kinds, not as independent validation; its rates are rates of judgment under the protocol. (b) Classroom's per-assignment template copies (name structure of the classroom slug, no author matching the repository name) are excluded as repositories; the name rule is checked on samples of 40 identified copies, 40 other verified repositories of the same period, and 20 earlier repositories against their public pages (number of forks; generated from a template) (`34_verify_copies.py`); record generation by half-year is reported (`28_record_generation.py`); two era contrasts restricted with respect to the platform change are added, keeping post-era repositories only if they were created before the general release of fork-based creation (2024-07-01) or before its beta (2024-01-01) (the restriction uses the repository's creation time, at which the creation method applies, while the era is still defined by the first classified commit; neither restriction guarantees a single creation method, because instructors could opt into the beta), and an agentic-era contrast limited to repositories without a Classroom-bot root commit of 20 or more lines. (c) Random-intercept models are fitted by an independent REML implementation (`reml_ri.py`) checked against statsmodels Nelder–Mead and BFGS (`26_validate_reml.py`), replacing the optimizer wrapper of E13. (d) Contribution-capped estimates are the mean of 200 draws; their 90% and 95% intervals come from an organization cluster bootstrap (1,000 replicates of five draws; 500 for sensitivity analyses and year effects); natural and standardized units come from the same fits; the multiple-imputation combination of E7 is kept only for comparison; stability is checked with 1,000 draws and a full repetition. (e) Within-organization intervals are obtained by inverting the restricted wild cluster bootstrap test; Webb six-point weights are used with fewer than 12 clusters. Where no null value was rejected on the grid of at least six bootstrap standard errors on each side, the inversion is repeated on a grid of 401 points extending 50 standard errors on each side (`within_wide`); where it again finds no bound, the searched range is reported instead of an interval. (f) The consistency rule of E7 and E12 is replaced by reporting the comparisons side by side and reading them from their range and intervals; an intermediate categorical reading (robust to weighting / same sign, imprecise / sensitive to weighting) is superseded, and signals are not assigned to categories. The within-organization comparison is reported separately, and a non-significant result is read as absence of evidence. (g) The share of repositories without classified commits is reported only for collected non-empty public repositories and is not read as participation or submission. (h) Starter code is flagged when a repository's root commit was made by the Classroom bot and adds 20 or more lines (`33_starter_code.py`), replacing an earlier ad hoc table that summed the lines of all bot-pattern commits. (i) Because the two reviews of staff-like exclusions disagree on their face, the staff-like category is decomposed by author breadth (authors in 50 or more repositories versus 5 to 49) and the two reviews are combined within each part (`29_activity_review_v4.py`); the resulting estimate (about one fifth judged student work) is the one used, and the stratum-weighted 46%, whose strata do not separate the two kinds of author, is reported with its explanation. (j) Message distinctiveness was computed in August 2026 from all non-bot messages, before the attribution rule existed, and could not be recomputed on classified commits because the embedding model was not available in the analysis environment; it is removed from the comparisons and reported as a supplementary result. The trailer analysis is likewise reported as a supplementary analysis. Implemented in `25_analysis_v4.py`, `27_trailer_v4.py`, `30_figs_v4.py`, `31_tables_v4.py`, `35_tables_v5.py`, and `36_figs_v5.py`. | A shared SHA is not proof of a template and a multi-repository author is not proof of staff (12% of the previous rule's inherited commits were committed after creation and were mostly students'; about one fifth of staff-like exclusions were judged students' work); Classroom's 2024 move to fork-based student repositories, combined with a search that excludes forks, removed most starter-code assignments from the collectable record and added the instructors' copies; the multiple-imputation rule has no justification for subsamples of one record; the earlier natural-unit and standardized intervals came from different draws; and comparisons that answer different questions had been treated as replications. |
